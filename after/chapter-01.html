Once

There was a room full of noise, and a version of us that believed it would stay that way. We built routines like promises. We mistook momentum for permanence.

We called it a plan. We called it forever. We were only wrong about the second part — everything, it turns out, is a draft.

Then, without ceremony —
it stopped.

No sound announced it.
Only its absence did.

What remains when the noise finally stops?

Not an echo. Not a scar. Something else — quieter, and still ours.

This is what remained: a single mark, faint but permanent — the shape a thing leaves behind after it has finished changing you. We started calling it the After.
